# The Math Track

Computer science is built on math, but most of the math it needs is
taught *inside* this course as it comes up (binary, logic, angles, Big-O).
This page lists the math the learner needs from **outside** the course,
and when.

Run the math track alongside the CS stages. If the math falls behind,
pause the CS stages at the gates below and do more of the math-flavored
optional projects in the meantime.

## Gates

| Before stage | Learner should be comfortable with | Taught where |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Python Turtle | Adding and subtracting within 1000; what "degrees" mean (the turtle teaches this) | Math curriculum + Stage 2 |
| 3 Python Games | Multiplication and division facts; remainders | Math curriculum |
| 4 Bits & Gates | Place value (ones, tens, hundreds); doubling | Math curriculum + Stage 0 dot cards |
| 6 Build a Computer | Negative numbers; powers of 2 up to 2¹⁶ | Math curriculum + Stage 4 |
| 7 Algorithms | Exponents; variables in expressions; reading graphs; a first idea of logarithms ("how many times can I halve this?") | Pre-algebra |
| 10 Operating Systems | Linear equations; functions | Algebra 1 |
| 11 Networks & Security | Modular ("clock") arithmetic; primes | Taught in Stage 11 + discrete math |
| 12 Languages & Data | Sets, logic, induction | Discrete math |

## Recommended free resources

All of these are free. Downloadable books can go in the offline library.

**Young learners (arithmetic to pre-algebra)**
- **Kolibri** (learningequality.org/kolibri): an open-source, fully
  *offline* learning platform with downloadable Khan Academy math
  channels. You install it on your computer, so nothing is online and
  there's no account with any outside service. Best fit for "offline first".
- **GCompris** math activities (installed in Stage 0).
- Numberblocks itself is great for place value and grouping. Revisit it
  when Stage 0's binary lesson and Stage 4 come around.

**Pre-algebra to precalculus (OpenStax, CC BY, free PDFs)**
These are college textbooks, so use them as a reference and a source of
problems for a young learner, and read alongside them.
- *Prealgebra 2e*: openstax.org/details/books/prealgebra-2e
- *Elementary Algebra 2e*: openstax.org/details/books/elementary-algebra-2e
- *Intermediate Algebra 2e*: openstax.org/details/books/intermediate-algebra-2e
- *College Algebra 2e*, then *Precalculus 2e*
- *Calculus Volumes 1 to 3* (optional, for graphics, physics and machine learning paths in Stage 13)

**Math for computer science**
- *Book of Proof*, Richard Hammack (free PDF): richardhammack.github.io/BookOfProof/.
  Chapters 1 to 6 before Stage 7's proof lesson.
- *Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction*, Oscar Levin (free, open):
  discrete.openmathbooks.org. Stages 10 to 12.
- *Mathematics for Computer Science*, Lehman, Leighton & Meyer (MIT, free PDF).
  The deep version, for Stage 12 and beyond.

**Math-flavored programming (no account needed to read the problems)**
- **Project Euler** problems (projecteuler.net/archives). Reading the problems
  needs no account. Checking answers online does, so check them by
  writing a second, slower solution instead. That's a great habit anyway.
