# Lesson 0.4: My Own Account

**Time:** 40 min · **You need:** the computer, with the learner's account
already made ([SETUP.md](../../docs/SETUP.md), step 3) · **New words:** user,
account, password, log in, log out, window, app

## Goal *(read this to the learner)*

> This computer has room for lots of people, and each person gets their
> own space called an **account**. Today you get YOUR account, with your
> own password, your own files, and your own desktop. Then you'll learn
> to drive it with the mouse.

## Before the lesson *(guide, 5 min)*

- [ ] Make sure the learner's account exists, and that you both know the
      password you chose together.
- [ ] Save anything open in your own account, then **log out** (top-right
      menu, then Log Out) so the login screen is showing.

## Warm-up (5 min)

"Remember the five jobs? When you type your password, what is the
keyboard doing? *(Input.)* When your name appears on the screen?
*(Output.)*"

## Activity

1. **Log in.** On the login screen, they click their own name and type
   their password. Point out that the password shows as dots: "So nobody
   peeking over your shoulder can see it."

2. **Password rules.** Talk through them:
   - Your password is a secret between you and your guide.
   - Never tell it to anyone else, not even friends.
   - Never type it into a website, a game or a message.

3. **Find and open apps.** Press the **Super key** (the one with the logo,
   bottom left). A search box appears. Type `gcompris` and press Enter.

4. **Windows.** With GCompris open, find the buttons in the top corner of
   its window:
   - **minimise** (hide it away; it's still running),
   - **maximise** (fill the screen),
   - **close** (✕).

   Practise: minimise it, then get it back (press Super, or click its icon).

5. **GCompris mouse and computer activities.** In GCompris, open the
   **computer** category (the one with a mouse or keyboard picture).
   Play the mouse activities: moving, clicking, double-clicking,
   dragging. Then let them explore any category for 10 minutes. The math
   and logic activities are great.

6. **Log out.** Top-right menu, then Log Out. Then log back in. Say: "When
   you're done with the computer, always log out, just like closing your
   front door."

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Sorry, that didn't work" at login | The password was typed wrong | Check that Caps Lock is off, and try again slowly. |
| Their name isn't on the login screen | The account wasn't made | Do [SETUP.md](../../docs/SETUP.md), step 3, from your account. |
| GCompris doesn't appear in search | Not installed | Run `./scripts/install-packages.sh` from your account. |
| Double-click is too hard | Normal at first | GCompris has a double-click activity. A few days of practice fixes it. |

## Talk about it

- Why does each person get their own account? *(Privacy; your files are
  yours, and nobody can mess up anyone else's things.)*
- Why do we keep passwords secret?
- Can you see your guide's files from your account? *(Try it in Lesson 0.5!)*

## Challenge

Open two apps at once (GCompris and Tux Paint). Switch between them
without closing either one. *(Super key, or Alt+Tab.)*

## Checkpoint

The learner can, without help:
- [ ] Log in and log out of their own account
- [ ] Open an app by searching for it, then minimise, maximise and close it

## Lab notebook

Write: "My account name is ______." (**NOT** the password!) Draw a window
and label the minimise, maximise and close buttons.

## Guide's background *(optional reading)*

Linux was built from the start for many users sharing one computer. Each
**user** has a home folder (`/home/theirname`) that other users can't
look inside, and only some users are allowed to change the system. The
learner's account isn't an admin on purpose: they can't accidentally
(or curiously!) break anything for the whole family. In Stage 5 they'll
learn exactly how this works, and get a separate computer where *they*
are the admin.
