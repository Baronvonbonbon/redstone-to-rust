# Lesson 0.3: Home Row Heroes

**Time:** 30 min · **You need:** the computer, logged into **your** account
for now (the learner gets theirs next lesson) · **New words:** home row,
touch typing

## Goal *(read this to the learner)*

> Programmers type a LOT. Super fast typists don't look at the keys.
> Their fingers know where to go. Today you'll start teaching your
> fingers the secret "home row".

## Before the lesson *(guide, 5 min)*

- [ ] Check that Tux Typing opens: press the Super key (the one with
      the Pop!_OS or Windows logo), type `tux typing`, and press Enter.
      Close it again.
- [ ] Set up a chair and table so their elbows are about level with the
      keyboard. A cushion or a box under their feet helps a lot.

## Warm-up (5 min)

Look at the keyboard together. Find the two keys with little bumps on
them: **F** and **J**. Say: "These bumps are so your fingers can find
home without looking, even in the dark."

## Activity

1. **Hands home.** Left fingers on `A S D F`, right fingers on `J K L ;`.
   Thumbs rest on the space bar. Index fingers on the bumps.

2. **Every finger has its own keys.** Show them: each finger reaches
   *up* or *down* from its home key, and comes back home. The index
   fingers also cover the middle column (`G`, `H`). Don't drill
   everything today; just home row.

3. **Play Tux Typing.** Open it (Super key, then type `tux typing`). Choose
   **Fish Cascade**, then the easiest level, and the **home row** letters
   if it asks. Play for 10 to 15 minutes.

   The rule: **look at the screen, not your hands.** Accuracy first;
   speed comes later all by itself.

4. **Try Klavaro too** *(optional)*. Open Klavaro, then **Basic course**,
   lesson 1. It's more like lessons than a game. Some kids prefer it.

## If something goes wrong

| What happens | What to do |
|---|---|
| Keeps peeking at the keys | Normal! Put a tea towel over their hands as a fun challenge, *after* they know the home row. |
| Typing with two pointer fingers | Gently move their hands back home. Expect this to take weeks. |
| Tux Typing won't open | Run `./scripts/install-packages.sh` from the setup again, or use Klavaro or GCompris's typing activities instead. |
| Getting frustrated | Stop at 10 minutes. Little and often wins. |

## Talk about it

- Why do you think the bumps are on F and J?
- How did it feel not to look? Did it get easier by the end?

## Challenge

Type `sad lad; ask dad; a fall; a flask` without looking. (Those use only
home-row keys!) Then make up your own home-row words.

## Checkpoint

The learner can, without help:
- [ ] Put their fingers on the home row without looking (using the bumps)

## Lab notebook

Draw the home row, and colour each finger's key a different colour.
Start a **typing log**: date, the game played, and the score or speed.

## Guide's background *(optional reading)*

From Stage 1 on, almost everything happens by typing commands. A learner
who hunts for every key spends their energy on finding letters instead
of thinking. Ten minutes a day of typing games for a few months makes
everything later much easier. Keep it in the daily free play until
they're typing comfortably. **GNU Typist** (`gtypist`, in the terminal)
is a good next step after Stage 1.
