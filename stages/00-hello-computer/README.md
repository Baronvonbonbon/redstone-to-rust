# Stage 0: Hello, Computer

**Badge:** Computer Whisperer · **Needs first:** reading, counting to 100 ·
**Lessons:** 11

Before typing a single command, the learner discovers the big ideas with
paper, cups, cards and their own body. A computer follows instructions
*exactly*. Everything inside it is numbers. Numbers can be written with
only two digits. Algorithms are recipes, and bugs are mistakes in the
recipe. Alongside that, they learn to type, use the mouse, log into
their own account, and save files into folders.

Lots of these lessons are "unplugged" (no computer). That's on purpose:
the ideas land better when you can hold them in your hands. They're
inspired by the wonderful CS Unplugged project (csunplugged.org).

## Free play for this stage

Between lessons, any time:
- **Typing:** Tux Typing (`tuxtype`) or Klavaro, 10 minutes a day. This matters more than it looks!
- **GCompris:** the computer-skills, logic, math and puzzle activities
- **Tux Paint:** make pictures and save them in the `lab` folder

## Supplies

- A paper notebook (the lab notebook) and pencils
- Index cards (at least 10), a marker, graph paper
- Two paper plates and a brass paper fastener (Lesson 0.10)
- 8 identical paper cups and some coins or pebbles (Lesson 0.8)
- Bread and a spread, or the other options in Lesson 0.1
- Masking tape for a floor grid (Lesson 0.9)
- Optional: an old computer you don't mind opening up (Lesson 0.2)

## Checkpoint (Lesson 0.11)

The learner can, without help:
- [ ] Log in to their own account and open an app
- [ ] Make a folder inside `lab`, and save a drawing into it
- [ ] Count from 0 to 15 in binary with the dot cards, and turn a binary number like `1011` into ordinary numbers
- [ ] Write step-by-step instructions that someone else can follow exactly, then find and fix a bug in them
- [ ] Explain in their own words what input, output and memory are
- [ ] Type the home row (asdf jkl;) without looking
