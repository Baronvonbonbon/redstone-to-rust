# Lesson 0.2: Meet the Machine

**Time:** 30–40 min · **You need:** the family computer (looking only), paper.
Optional: an old computer, phone or keyboard you don't mind opening, and a
small screwdriver · **New words:** input, output, processor (CPU), memory,
storage

## Goal *(read this to the learner)*

> Every computer, from a giant one to the one in a toaster, has the
> same five jobs. Today we'll find each one, and if we're lucky, we'll
> look *inside* a computer!

## Before the lesson *(guide, 10 min)*

- [ ] Read this lesson once.
- [ ] If you have an old computer to open: **unplug it, and take out any
      battery.** Never open anything plugged in, and never open a power
      supply (the metal box where the power cable goes in; it can hold a
      shock even when unplugged). Old desktop PCs are the easiest: the
      side panel usually comes off with two screws.
- [ ] No old computer? Search for "inside a desktop computer" pictures
      on your own beforehand and print one, or simply draw one.
- [ ] Draw the five-jobs chart (below) on a big sheet of paper.

## Warm-up (5 min)

"Remember the sandwich robot? The robot needed to *hear* the
instructions, *think* about them, *remember* what step it was on, and
*do* things with its hands. A computer needs the same things."

## Activity

1. **Draw the five jobs** on a big sheet of paper, with a picture for each:

   ```
   INPUT ──▶ PROCESSOR ──▶ OUTPUT
   (ears &    (brain:      (mouth &
    eyes)     thinking)     hands)
                 │
         ┌───────┴────────┐
       MEMORY          STORAGE
    (remembering    (writing it in
     for right now)  a notebook, so it's
                     kept forever)
   ```

2. **Hunt for input and output.** Walk around the family computer and
   label things with sticky notes:
   - Input (things that tell the computer stuff): keyboard, mouse,
     microphone, camera, touchscreen
   - Output (things the computer uses to tell *us* stuff): screen,
     speakers, printer, and the little lights on the keyboard

3. **The tricky one: memory vs storage.** Say: "Memory is like
   remembering a phone number just long enough to dial it. Storage is
   like writing it in your notebook. If the power goes out, the computer
   forgets everything in memory, but storage keeps it."
   - Ask: "When you turn a computer off and back on, are your drawings
     still there? *(Yes!)* So where are they kept?" *(Storage.)*

4. **Look inside** *(if you have an old machine)*. Open it together. Find:
   - The **processor (CPU)**: usually under a big metal fan or heat sink.
     "It gets hot because it's thinking SO fast: billions of tiny steps
     every second."
   - **Memory (RAM)**: long thin sticks clipped into slots.
   - **Storage**: a box (hard drive) or a flat stick or card (SSD), with
     a cable.
   - The **motherboard**: the big board everything plugs into. Look at
     the tiny lines on it. "Those are roads for electricity carrying
     numbers between the parts."

   Let them touch the parts (unplugged, with clean, dry hands). Pull out
   a RAM stick together if it unclips easily.

5. **Sort these** onto the chart. Ask "is this input, output, processing,
   memory or storage?" for each one:
   keyboard, speaker, USB stick, screen, microphone, "the computer is
   adding numbers", a photo saved on the computer, game controller, printer.

## If something goes wrong

| What happens | What to do |
|---|---|
| The screws won't come out | Skip opening it. A picture works just as well today. |
| "Is a touchscreen input or output?" | Great question: it's both! Lots of things are. |
| Mixing up memory and storage | That's very normal. Use the notebook example again; it'll come back in later stages. |

## Talk about it

- Which part does the thinking? *(The processor, or CPU.)*
- What happens to memory when the power goes out? *(It's forgotten.)*
- Your brain does all five jobs. Which part of you is input? Output?

## Challenge

Pick something in the house that has a computer inside (a microwave, a
washing machine, a game console). Name its inputs and outputs. *(Microwave:
buttons in; screen, beeps and the spinning plate out.)*

## Checkpoint

The learner can, without help:
- [ ] Name two inputs and two outputs on the family computer
- [ ] Explain the difference between memory and storage in their own words

## Lab notebook

Draw the five-jobs chart from memory, with one example for each job.

## Guide's background *(optional reading)*

Every general-purpose computer has these parts. The **CPU** follows
instructions, one tiny step at a time (adding two numbers, or copying a
number from one place to another), billions of times a second. **RAM**
holds the program and data the CPU is working on right now; it's fast,
but it's wiped when the power goes off. **Storage** (an SSD or hard
drive) is slower but permanent; it holds files and programs while the
computer is off.

In Stage 6 your learner will build every one of these parts, as a
simulated circuit, starting from the simplest possible logic gate. This
lesson plants the seed.
